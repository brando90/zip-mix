\section{Exp.\ 07: a null result for stratified Zip-Mix selection}
\label{app:exp07-null}
In a pre-registered calibration run, selecting training packs by Zip-Mix alignment within fixed source$\times$compression strata gave no detectable gain on the held-out target accuracy or on retention, relative to shuffled-alignment and uniform selection.
We found no data or pipeline bug. The stratification held the source mix identical across selectors (SciQ 30.5\%), while the alignment score is largely a SciQ detector (correlation 0.89 with the SciQ indicator); the selectors therefore differed only in within-domain ranking, by 0.07 pool standard deviations in mean alignment, and their predictions differed from each other no more than two seeds of the same arm do (5.6\% disagreement).
A post hoc, exploratory sweep (not pre-registered; 5 seeds, learning rates $2\times10^{-6}$ and $2\times10^{-5}$) shows that unstratified selection also does not help: tilting the draw to 73\% SciQ changed target accuracy by $+0.8$ pp (95\% CI $[-1.1,+2.8]$) at $2\times10^{-6}$, and retention fell by 1.4 pp ($[-2.2,-0.6]$). Even the 100\%-SciQ oracle gained only $+1.7$ pp ($[-0.2,+3.7]$) over uniform, and nothing at $2\times10^{-5}$, where fine-tuning already hurt the base model. Training on anti-aligned data did hurt (up to $-5.9$ pp at $2\times10^{-5}$), so data quality can lower accuracy in this recipe, but we observe no gain from better data.
With five seeds the sweep resolves differences of roughly 2 pp on target accuracy (seed-level paired standard error about 0.7 pp), so it is adequate for the original 2 pp bar but cannot rule out effects below it; the null should be read as ``no detectable benefit at this model and step budget,'' not as evidence against Zip-Mix in settings with more headroom.
